# Edge-Inversion Diagnostic — Findings & Hypotheses

Status: **analysis only**. No model formula, threshold, weight, prior,
edge cutoff, or recommendation logic was changed to produce this
document. Methodology: `lib/research/hitter_edge_diagnostic.py`; machine-
readable output: `edge_inversion_diagnostic.json`; both regenerated by
`scripts/research/build_edge_inversion_diagnostic.py` against the
current `data/edgelab/hitter_validation` audit corpus (currently N=1,783
primary rows, 3 calendar dates, 8 distinct game-dates, 125 distinct
player-dates — see `summary.md` §12 and `provenance_audit.json`).

## The finding under investigation

The retrospective audit (`summary.md` §6) found declared edge
(`|modelProbability − executableKalshiPrice|`) above roughly 5
percentage points is an **anti-signal**: larger declared edge correlates
with *worse* calibration and *worse* simulated ROI, not better.

| Cohort | N | Calibration error | Simulated ROI |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Small edge (<5pp) | 1,093 | (well-calibrated, see `summary.md` §6) | **+11.0%** |
| **Large edge (≥5pp)** | **690** | **overconfident** | **−18.7%** |

## Does this survive de-correlation? — **Yes.**

Raw row count overstates independent evidence here (many rows share the
same underlying game/hitter outcome). This diagnostic re-weights the
large-edge cohort by cluster instead of by row, two different ways:

| Weighting | N (rows) | Distinct clusters | Mean net P/L per unit | % of clusters net-negative |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Row-weighted | 690 | — | **−0.187** | — |
| Cluster-weighted by (date, player) | 690 | 124 | **−0.120** | **58.1%** |
| Cluster-weighted by (date, game) | 690 | 8 | **−0.131** | **62.5%** |

All three weightings are negative, same sign, same order of magnitude.
The majority of individual hitter-date clusters (58%) and game-date
clusters (63%) are themselves net-negative — this is not two or three
outlier hitters or one bad slate dragging down an otherwise-fine
average; it is a broad-based pattern across the large-edge cohort.
**Conclusion: the large-edge underperformance is not a decorrelation
artifact.**

That said, 8 distinct game-dates is still a very small cluster count —
"survives de-correlation" here means "is not obviously an artifact of a
handful of outliers," not "is a statistically air-tight finding
independent of season-level replication." See Data Gaps below.

## Breakdown by market family

| Family | N | Calib. error (large-edge only) | ROI (large-edge only) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| `hitter_total_bases` | 148 | −0.5pp | **−32.3%** |
| `hitter_hits` | 184 | **−13.6pp** | −23.6% |
| `hitter_hits_runs_rbis` | 236 | −4.0pp | −19.3% |
| `hitter_rbis` | 122 | +3.7pp | **+6.1%** |

Every family except `hitter_rbis` is negative at large edge.
`hitter_total_bases` has the single worst large-edge ROI (−32.3%)
despite near-zero calibration error at this slice — its damage looks
more like a pricing/fee-structure effect than a pure probability-
overconfidence effect (see the executable-price-bucket breakdown below).
`hitter_rbis` is the one family where large declared edge is still
directionally useful, echoing the same family's strong overall CLV
finding in the primary audit (`summary.md` §5) — worth flagging as the
one place in this corpus where "trust the model's edge" doesn't yet look
wrong.

## Breakdown by probability bucket

| Bucket | N | Calib. error | ROI |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| <35% | 442 | +0.5pp | −18.0% |
| 35–49.9% | 87 | −9.1pp | −40.0% |
| 50–64.9% | 31 | −15.4pp | +3.5% (n too small to trust) |
| 65–79.9% | 128 | −15.0pp | −12.0% |
| 80%+ | 2 | — | — (n=2, noise) |

Note the contrast with the primary audit's own <35% bucket (`summary.md`
§4/§6): across ALL edges, <35% is the best-calibrated, most profitable
bucket. Restricted to LARGE edge specifically, the SAME probability
bucket turns ROI-negative (−18.0%), even though its calibration error
stays small (+0.5pp). **This means the <35% bucket's overall good
performance is being carried by its SMALL-edge rows; its large-edge
rows are not actually good.** This is a genuinely new, more precise
finding than the primary audit's bucket-level view alone could show.

## Tail vs. non-tail thresholds

"Tail" = the top 2 threshold rungs actually observed for that market
family (e.g. 3+/4+ hits, not a fixed absolute number — families have
different natural ranges).

| | N | Calib. error | ROI |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| NON_TAIL | 572 | −6.5pp | −16.5% |
| TAIL | 118 | **+5.6pp** | **−29.6%** |

This is a genuinely counter-intuitive result: at large declared edge,
TAIL rungs are actually **slightly underconfident** (positive
calibration error), not overconfident — yet they have the WORSE ROI of
the two. Calibration error alone does not explain the tail-threshold
loss; see the executable-price-bucket finding below for the more likely
mechanism.

## Executable-price bucket — the standout finding

| Price bucket | N | Calib. error | ROI |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| <10¢ | 39 | −6.5pp | **−97.4%** |
| 10–29¢ | 318 | −3.5pp | −17.6% |
| 30–49¢ | 174 | +1.2pp | −13.0% |
| 50–69¢ | 150 | −11.5pp | −6.9% |
| 70–89¢ | 9 | — | (n too small) |

The <10¢ bucket (very cheap "longshot YES" contracts, large declared
edge on top of an already-long price) is catastrophic: −97.4% simulated
ROI on 39 rows. Its calibration error (−6.5pp) is real but far too small
in isolation to explain a near-total-loss ROI — a longshot contract's
P/L is extremely sensitive to exactly this kind of small absolute
overconfidence (predicting 8% when the truth is 2% is a small
calibration-error number but a ~4x overstatement of true win
probability). **This looks like the dominant mechanism, not tail
thresholds specifically or market family specifically** — tail
thresholds and certain families (`hitter_total_bases`) are simply where
cheap, longshot-priced contracts concentrate.

## Lineup slot

| Slot | N | Calib. error | ROI |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| TOP_1_3 | 298 | +3.3pp | −14.5% |
| MIDDLE_4_6 | 216 | −6.0pp | −15.3% |
| BOTTOM_7_9 | 176 | **−15.6pp** | **−30.1%** |

Bottom-of-the-order hitters show the worst calibration AND worst ROI at
large declared edge. A plausible mechanism: bottom-order hitters more
often have thinner recent-performance samples (see
`sampleSizeDiagnostics.hitterArchivedPACount` on individual board rows,
not yet cross-tabulated in this pass — a natural follow-up), which the
shrinkage/Monte Carlo engine may not be discounting enough when
`hitterArchivedPACount` is low, producing noisier and occasionally
extreme probability estimates.

## Home / away

Away hitters: calib. error −1.3pp, ROI −21.6% (N=365). Home hitters:
calib. error −7.9pp, ROI −15.5% (N=325). Directionally similar, no
dramatic asymmetry — not flagged as a leading hypothesis.

## Net executable edge (fee-adjusted)

Re-bucketing by edge net of the fee-adjusted break-even price (rather
than the raw price) doesn't change the picture: every band from 2pp to
20pp is negative (−17.1% to −20.0% ROI); the fee adjustment itself is
not what's driving the anti-signal — it persists on a fee-aware basis
too.

## By player (N ≥ 3 large-edge rows, 87 qualifying players)

Genuinely mixed, not dominated by one or two names: of the 10
highest-volume players, 4 are net positive (Ezequiel Duran +56.5%,
Brandon Nimmo +17.0%, Shohei Ohtani +11.5%, Jac Caglianone +2.3%) and 6
are net negative, several severely (Jake Burger −57.4%, Lawrence Butler
−46.0%, Wyatt Langford −36.7%, Mike Trout −31.0%). This spread is
consistent with the concentration check above: the pattern is broad
enough across players that it isn't reducible to "one hitter's model is
broken."

## What this points to (hypotheses, not conclusions — no tuning performed)

Ranked by how directly the evidence above supports each:

1. **Overconfidence is concentrated in cheap, longshot-priced contracts
   (<10-30¢), independent of which market family or threshold they
   belong to.** The <10¢ bucket's −97% ROI on real, non-tiny N is the
   single most striking number in this diagnostic. A small absolute
   miscalibration at a long price is proportionally enormous.
2. **Bottom-of-the-order hitters are where large-edge overconfidence is
   worst**, plausibly tied to thinner recent-sample sizes feeding the
   Monte Carlo engine's shrinkage — not yet directly cross-tabulated
   against `sampleSizeDiagnostics.hitterArchivedPACount`, a natural next
   analysis pass.
3. **Tail thresholds are not, by themselves, the driver** — their
   calibration error is actually the least bad of any large-edge slice
   examined; their poor ROI is better explained by hypothesis 1 (tail
   rungs are disproportionately priced cheap).
4. **This is not a fee-structure artifact** — the pattern persists
   identically whether measured against raw edge or fee-adjusted net
   edge.
5. **This is not a single-game or single-player artifact** — it
   survives cluster-weighting by both (date, player) and (date, game),
   and is spread across a majority of clusters in both groupings.

## Data gaps that limit this diagnostic

- No sportsbook/reference price exists anywhere in this repository's
  archived hitter-prop data (confirmed during the primary audit) — the
  "sportsbook/reference price disagreement" dimension requested for this
  diagnostic cannot be computed; there is nothing to compare Kalshi's
  price against.
- No real snapshot-timing diversity exists yet (see `summary.md` §11) —
  a "does this pattern differ by T-90 vs T-30 vs closing" cut is not yet
  possible; `snapshot_timing.json` will become informative once
  `lib.research.hitter_prospective_snapshot`'s scheduled checkpoint
  system has accumulated real multi-checkpoint data.
- `sampleSizeDiagnostics.hitterArchivedPACount` (a genuine measure of
  how much real history each row's simulation had) was NOT cross-
  tabulated in this pass — flagged in hypothesis 2 as the most promising
  immediate follow-up.
- Only 3 calendar dates / 8 game-dates total. Every finding above,
  including the de-correlation check, should be re-run and checked for
  replication once the new checkpoint-scheduler system
  (`lib/research/hitter_prospective_snapshot.py`) has accumulated
  substantially more independent slates.
