## The finding under investigation

The retrospective audit (`summary.md` §6) found declared edge
(`|modelProbability − executableKalshiPrice|`) above roughly 5
percentage points is an **anti-signal**: larger declared edge correlates
with *worse* calibration and *worse* simulated ROI, not better.

| Cohort | N | Simulated ROI | Before settlement |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Small edge (<5pp) | 1,145 | **+8.17%** | N=1,093, +11.0% |
| **Large edge (≥5pp)** | **725** | **−19.54%** | N=690, −18.7% |

## Does this survive de-correlation? — **Still yes.**

Raw row count overstates independent evidence here (many rows share the
same underlying game/hitter outcome). This diagnostic re-weights the
large-edge cohort by cluster instead of by row, two different ways:

| Weighting | N (rows) | Distinct clusters | Mean net P/L per unit | % of clusters net-negative |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Row-weighted | 725 | — | **−0.195** | — |
| Cluster-weighted by (date, player) | 725 | 137 | **−0.146** | **59.9%** |
| Cluster-weighted by (date, game) | 725 | 9 | **−0.156** | **66.7%** |

(Before settlement: 690 rows, 124/8 clusters, −0.120/−0.131 mean net
P/L, 58.1%/62.5% net-negative — the pattern strengthened slightly on
every one of these numbers after adding 2026-08-14.)

All three weightings are still negative, same sign, same order of
magnitude. The majority of individual hitter-date clusters (now 60%)
and game-date clusters (now 67%) are themselves net-negative — this is
still not two or three outlier hitters or one bad slate dragging down an
otherwise-fine average; it is a broad-based pattern across the
large-edge cohort, and it got slightly more broad-based, not less, with
more data. **Conclusion unchanged: the large-edge underperformance is
not a decorrelation artifact.**

That said, 9 distinct game-dates is still a very small cluster count —
"survives de-correlation" here means "is not obviously an artifact of a
handful of outliers," not "is a statistically air-tight finding
independent of season-level replication." See Data Gaps below.

## What this points to (hypotheses, not conclusions — no tuning performed)

Ranked by how directly the evidence above supports each:

1. **Overconfidence is concentrated in cheap, longshot-priced contracts
   (<10-30¢), independent of which market family or threshold they
   belong to.** The <10¢ bucket's −97% ROI on real, non-tiny N is the
   single most striking number in this diagnostic. A small absolute
   miscalibration at a long price is proportionally enormous.
2. **Bottom-of-the-order hitters are where large-edge overconfidence is
   worst**, plausibly tied to thinner recent-sample sizes feeding the
   Monte Carlo engine's shrinkage — not yet directly cross-tabulated
   against `sampleSizeDiagnostics.hitterArchivedPACount`, a natural next
   analysis pass.
3. **Tail thresholds are not, by themselves, the driver** — their
   calibration error is actually the least bad of any large-edge slice
   examined; their poor ROI is better explained by hypothesis 1 (tail
   rungs are disproportionately priced cheap).
4. **This is not a fee-structure artifact** — the pattern persists
   identically whether measured against raw edge or fee-adjusted net
   edge.
5. **This is not a single-game or single-player artifact** — it
   survives cluster-weighting by both (date, player) and (date, game),
   and is spread across a majority of clusters in both groupings.

## Data gaps that limit this diagnostic

- No sportsbook/reference price exists anywhere in this repository's
  archived hitter-prop data (confirmed during the primary audit) — the
  "sportsbook/reference price disagreement" dimension requested for this
  diagnostic cannot be computed; there is nothing to compare Kalshi's
  price against.
- No real snapshot-timing diversity exists yet (see `summary.md` §11) —
  a "does this pattern differ by T-90 vs T-30 vs closing" cut is not yet
  possible; `snapshot_timing.json` will become informative once
  `lib.research.hitter_prospective_snapshot`'s scheduled checkpoint
  system has accumulated real multi-checkpoint data.
- `sampleSizeDiagnostics.hitterArchivedPACount` (a genuine measure of
  how much real history each row's simulation had) was NOT cross-
  tabulated in this pass — flagged in hypothesis 2 as the most promising
  immediate follow-up.
- Only 4 calendar dates / 9 game-dates total (was 3/8 — 2026-08-14
  settled 2026-08-18, reflected throughout this document). Every
  finding above, including the de-correlation check, should continue to
  be re-run and checked for replication as the newly-live
  checkpoint-scheduler system (`lib/research/hitter_prospective_snapshot.py`,
  `.github/workflows/hitter-snapshot-scheduler.yml`, merged in this same
  PR) accumulates substantially more independent slates automatically.
